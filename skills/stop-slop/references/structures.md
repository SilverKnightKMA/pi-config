# Structural patterns and exceptions

## Negative parallelism ("not X, but Y")

"**Not because X. Because Y.**", "X isn't the problem. Y is.", "The answer isn't X. It's Y.", "It feels like X. It's actually Y.", "not just X but also Y" — state Y directly.

**Exception (genuine contrast):** keep the form only when the second clause adds new information the reader could not infer. Test: delete the first clause; if nothing is lost, the contrast was runway, not content.

## Negative listing

"Not a X. Not a Y. A Z." / "It wasn't X. It wasn't Y. It was Z." — state Z.

## Dramatic fragmentation

"[Noun]. That's it. That's the [thing]." / "X. And Y. And Z." — fragments used as manufactured profundity. Use complete sentences; do not stack punchy fragments.

## Rhetorical setups

"What if [reframe]?", "Here's what I mean:", "Think about it:", "And that's okay." — make the point; let the reader draw conclusions.

## Triads (rule of three)

LLMs overuse three-item lists where the third item restates the first two at a grander register. Prefer one or two concrete items; cut the third when it is a summary in disguise.

## Em-dash pivot (pattern rule, not a character ban)

Em dashes are legitimate in technical parentheticals. The tell is the *pivot*: a mid-sentence dash that relaunches at a grander register — the "not-X-but-Y" move in costume ("The tool is fast — and that changes everything."). Rewrite the pivot with a comma, colon, or period. Spaced em dashes are the stronger tell.

## False agency (name the actor)

"a complaint becomes a fix", "the decision emerges", "the culture shifts", "the data tells us", "the market rewards" — name the person, team, or group that acts. Scope: marketing-style anthropomorphism only; "the parser returns None" is correct technical prose.

## Passive voice (scoped)

"X was created" → name who created X. "It is believed that" → name who believes. Legitimate technical passives that hide no actor ("the buffer is flushed") pass.

## Rhythm traps

- Three consecutive sentences of matching length → break one.
- Paragraphs ending in a steady run of punchy one-liners → vary the endings.
- Questions answered immediately by the next sentence → drop the question.
- "So," paragraph starts and "Look," sentence starts, unless dialogue.
